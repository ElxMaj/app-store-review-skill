One front door

Three ways into App Review

The skill finds the project type, gathers deterministic evidence, and keeps confirmed facts separate from manual checks and inference.

01

Pre-submission audit

Check native targets, permissions, privacy manifests, purchases, account deletion, metadata, reviewer access, and release paths before upload.

02

Rejection recovery

Preserve Apple’s exact message, classify the response as fix, clarify, appeal, or request interpretation, then draft a precise reply.

03

Human-craft audit

Review distinction, provenance, visual identity, microcopy, missing states, accessibility, and the App Store product page.

What Apple publishes

Apple’s published guidelines do not name AI-written code as a rejection category

Apple’s published rules focus on the app that ships: useful functionality, distinct value, truthful metadata, privacy, safety, and who submits template-generated work.

Read the guide to Guidelines 4.2.6 and 4.3

Limits

What the skill does not claim

A useful review keeps unknowns visible. It does not turn a repository scan into a promise from Apple.

Does it guarantee App Store approval?

No. It finds evidence and prepares review work, but Apple makes the decision.

Does it detect AI-written code?

No. It reviews product evidence, policy risks, and app quality. It does not claim to detect AI-written code.

Does it edit my project?

The first pass is read-only. You see the report before any proposed fix group is applied.
